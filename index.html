About

Research grounded in real financial decisions

I am a Lecturer in Finance at the University of North Texas, where I completed my Ph.D. in Finance in 2026. My research interests include corporate governance, agency conflicts, ownership structure, shareholder activism, and ESG and sustainable investing.

Across my research, I examine how information and governance mechanisms influence institutional investors and market outcomes. I pair careful empirical design with data engineering, econometrics, and natural language processing to study questions that matter to scholars, investors, and firms.

Before entering academia, I worked as a Chartered Accountant and financial consultant in India. That experience continues to shape how I connect financial theory, institutional detail, and practical decision-making in both my research and teaching.

Job Market Paper

ESG Ratings, Institutional Rebalancing, and Index Mechanics

I examine whether changes in institutional ownership around MSCI ESG rating events reflect discretionary responses to sustainability information or portfolio adjustments associated with index rules. The study combines MSCI ESG ratings, quarterly SEC Form 13F holdings, and ESG-focused ETF holdings for U.S. firms from 2013 to 2023.

Rating upgrades are associated with increased institutional ownership, with dedicated investors driving the response. The negative ownership estimate around ESG ETF deletions becomes statistically insignificant after accounting for concurrent exits from parent broad-market indexes. These findings show why benchmark construction must be considered when interpreting investor responses to ESG signals.

Working Paper

ESG Ratings, Ownership, and Stock Returns: Cross-Country Analysis

Using MSCI ratings and holdings from 2,585 ESG-focused open-end equity funds, this project studies investor responses across countries. The evidence is strongest at the fund-participation margin and around the BB index-eligibility threshold.

Work in Progress

Governance by Contract: Shareholder Agreements and Corporate Control

This solo-authored project uses SEC EDGAR filings and NLP-assisted clause extraction to construct measures of board representation, voting, transfer, and other contractual control rights not captured by conventional ownership measures. It examines how contractual rights alter the relationship between cash-flow ownership, voting power, and corporate decisions.

  • SEC filings

Teaching

Building confidence through questions and application

Read teaching statement ↗

I believe good teaching begins with the right questions. I ask students to test ideas, explain their reasoning, and view difficult material as an opportunity to grow.

In Introduction to Finance, I frame concepts for both finance majors and students from other fields: professional applications for one audience and personal financial decisions for another. Group activities, peer discussion, and small problem-solving sessions help students build confidence and learn from one another.
