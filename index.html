About

My research examines how institutional ownership and sustainability considerations influence firm value, combining empirical corporate finance with modern data engineering and natural language processing techniques.

Job Market Paper

ESG Ratings, Ownership Structure, and Firm Value

I examine how MSCI Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) rating changes affect institutional ownership and stock returns in U.S. firms from 2013 to 2023. I focus on three observable ownership shocks: ESG rating upgrades and downgrades, rating changes crossing MSCI's BB threshold for ESG‑index inclusion, and firms added to or removed from ESG‑focused exchange‑traded funds (ETFs). For identification, I use two‑way fixed effects, propensity‑score matching, difference‑in‑differences, and MSCI's 2012Q4 mass coverage initiation of 1,602 firms as a quasi‑natural experiment. Using these methods and event‑study tests, I find that ESG upgrades are associated with a modest 0.203% increase in institutional ownership in the baseline model, while the evidence for downgrades is stronger in matched and DiD specifications. BB downgrades and ESG ETF removals show the clearest negative ownership patterns, with ETF removals associated with a 1.361% decline in 13F holdings. Stock‑price reactions to rating changes are generally small and sensitive to the abnormal‑return model, although downgrades followed by ETF removals are associated with negative abnormal returns. Coverage initiation is followed by a 1.16% increase in institutional ownership, consistent with an ESG visibility channel. Overall, the results suggest that ESG ratings are associated with institutional ownership changes primarily through visibility, screening, and exclusion‑related channels, rather than through broad immediate valuation effects.

Working Papers

ESG Ratings, Ownership and Stock Returns — Cross‑Country Analysis

This paper examines the relation between MSCI ESG rating changes, ESG‑focused ownership, and stock returns globally. Using MSCI ESG rating changes and the holdings data of 2,585 ESG‑focused open‑end equity funds from Morningstar Direct, this paper finds that ESG‑fund ownership is higher among better‑rated firms, rising from 0.13% of shares outstanding for CCC‑rated firms to 0.83% for AAA‑rated firms, and firms above the BB index‑eligibility cutoff are held more heavily than firms at or below BB. However, rating changes are not associated with the expected short‑run increase in aggregate ownership percentage: upgrades show a small negative ownership coefficient, while downgrades are not statistically significant. A propensity‑score‑matched difference‑in‑differences analysis provides similar evidence, showing no robust ownership‑percentage association around rating events within the six‑month event window. The evidence is stronger at the fund‑participation margin, where downgrades are associated with 0.1349 fewer ESG‑focused fund holders per firm‑month and BB downgrades with 0.2316 fewer fund holders. Stock‑return evidence is small on average but more visible around the BB threshold: U.S. BB upgrades are associated with positive abnormal returns of roughly 0.2% to 0.6%, while BB downgrades in Australia and Switzerland are associated with negative abnormal returns of roughly 0.7% to 1.5%. Overall, ESG rating changes are most informative around the BB index‑eligibility threshold, and fund participation captures investor response better than aggregate ownership.

Work in Progress
  • Governance by Contract: Shareholder Agreements and Corporate Control

    This project examines how markets perceive shareholder agreements as governance mechanisms. I exploit two opposing exogenous shocks—a Delaware court ruling (Moelis) that invalidated such agreements if not included in the charter, and a subsequent legislative proposal to overturn the ruling—to study market reactions. I have built a data pipeline using the SEC API to collect shareholder agreements, and preliminary event‑study analysis is complete. Ongoing work focuses on leveraging a large language model (LLM) to automatically classify the powers granted to shareholders—an approach that allows for scalable and accurate analysis—and refining how market reactions vary based on these contractual rights.

  • Activism by Blockholders (with Stuart Gillan, Daniel Rettl & Vivek Acharya)
Research Statement

My research interest is in corporate finance, with a focus on corporate governance, agency conflicts, ownership structure, activism, and how ESG integrates with these broader concepts. These themes guide all of my work, and you can see them in both my completed and ongoing papers.

  • Job Market Paper: Examines the causal effect of ESG ratings on institutional ownership and firm value, using rigorous empirical approaches such as propensity score matching and difference-in-differences to identify the impact of ESG changes on investor behavior and market valuation.
  • Working Paper: Extends these questions to a global setting, using MSCI ESG ratings and Morningstar Direct holdings data for 2,585 ESG‑focused funds to compare how institutional environments across countries influence the link between ownership, ESG, and firm value, with the clearest effects concentrated around the BB index‑eligibility threshold.
  • Work in Progress: Governance by Contract: Shareholder Agreements and Corporate Control uses SEC contract data and large language models to study how shareholder agreements and legal shocks shape governance outcomes. Activism by Blockholders (with Stuart Gillan, Daniel Rettl & Vivek Acharya) studies how large shareholders exert influence over corporate control and firm outcomes.

Future Directions

Going forward, my research philosophy is to combine rigorous empirical methods with modern technology to explore important questions in corporate finance. I am guided by curiosity and a commitment to hard work, and I hope to continue producing research that is both empirically sound and contributes to a deeper understanding of corporate behavior.
